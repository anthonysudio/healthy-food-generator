Nutrition Principles

Building Better Eating Habits

Healthy eating does not have to be a chore. Here are some guidelines to help you maintain a balanced diet.

Avoid

Foods to Avoid or Have on Rare Occasions

  • Sodium: High Sodium forces the body to hold onto extra water. This causes more strain on the heart and blood vessels. This is one of the main contributors to high blood pressure.
  • Trans Fats: Trans fats raise bad cholesterol (LDL) and lower good cholesterol (HDL), increasing the risk of heart disease.
  • Artificial Additives and Preservatives: Some additives and preservatives may have negative effects on health, and can also be carcinogenic.
  • Sugar: Excessive sugar consumption can increase risk of type 2 diabetes and heart disease.
Limit

Saturated Fats & Sodium

  • Red & Processed Meats: Eating red meat in moderation is acceptable. However minimizing processed meats is highly recommended, as they include excessive sodium and unhealthy fats.
  • Saturated Fats: Saturated fats can raise bad cholesterol (LDL) and increase the risk of heart disease.
  • Alcohol: Excessive alcohol consumption can lead to liver damage, high blood pressure, and increased risk of certain cancers.
  • Full-Fat Dairy: Choose low-fat to reduce saturated fat levels.
Include

Whole Foods & Fiber

  • Vegetables: Leafy greens, broccoli, and root vegetables supply crucial micronutrients, antioxidants and are low in calories.
  • Fruits: Fruits have natural vitamins and fiber.
  • High-Fiber Staples: Beans, lentils, oats, and whole grains stabilize blood sugar levels.
  • Healthy Fats: Extra virgin olive oil, avocados, and nuts support heart and brain function.
  • Lean Proteins: Poultry, seafood, eggs, tofu, and legumes for protein.
Maintain

Daily Health Targets

  • Sodium: Keep daily intake under 2,000 mg.
  • Saturated Fat: Limit to less than 10% of your total daily calories.
  • Dietary Fibre: Target 25g to 30g daily from whole foods.
  • Added Sugars: Restrict to under 5% to 10% of daily energy.
  • Hydration: Aim for 2 to 2.5 litres of water daily to maintain metabolic efficiency.
